4//! # The Linera protocol: correctness specification
5//!
6//! Linera is a multi-chain protocol: state is partitioned into *microchains*, each running its own
7//! consensus instance per block height. No chain can read another's state; they communicate by
8//! explicit message passing and through shared immutable stores — today, published blobs and events.
9//! This crate is the entry point to a correctness specification of that protocol — its system
10//! model, its assumptions, and proofs of the properties it is meant to have.
11//!
12//! The specification is written subsystem by subsystem; [Coverage](#coverage) says what is
13//! established today and what is not yet constrained by any statement here.
14//!
15//! **This crate contains no code.** The statements live next to the implementation they describe —
16//! today across [`linera_chain`] and [`linera_core`] — and this crate exists only so that a single
17//! index can link to all of them. Neither of those crates can do that on its own —
18//! `linera-core` depends on `linera-chain`, so the chain crate cannot name the progress and
19//! liveness results, and an index living there would have to describe half the specification in
20//! prose. Sitting above both, this crate can cite every statement by path, so every cross
21//! reference below is checked by the documentation build.
22//!
23//! ```bash
24//! cargo doc -p linera-spec --open
25//! ```
26//!
27//! # Headline results
28//!
29//! Three results anchor the consensus core, each concerning one microchain's sequence of blocks.
30//!
31//! * **Safety** — [`CommitAgreement`]: for any chain and height, all valid confirmed block
32//!   certificates certify the same block. No synchrony, availability or fairness assumption is
33//!   used; only [`MaxByzantineWeight`] and the cryptographic and persistence assumptions.
34//! * **Accountability** — [`AccountableSafety`]: if agreement *does* fail, the two conflicting
35//!   certificates alone convict validators of weight at least [`validity_threshold`] — more than
36//!   [`MaxByzantineWeight`] permits — and no correct validator is ever convictable. It assumes
37//!   strictly less than safety does, since it must hold precisely where safety does not.
38//! * **Liveness** — [`UnboundedProgress`]: with an active correct client and after GST, every
39//!   correct reachable validator's [`ChainTipState::next_block_height`] grows without bound.
40//!

64//! # How to read a statement
65//!
66//! Every claim is a public marker trait with no members, no implementors and no runtime
67//! footprint. Its **name is its identity**; its doc comment carries the statement and, unless it
68//! is a definition or an assumption, a proof. **Supertraits are proof dependencies**: a statement
69//! lists as supertraits exactly the earlier statements whose truth its proof consumes.
70//!
71//! ```text
72//! pub trait RoundProgress:
73//!     EventuallyCorrectLeader + LockRecovery + ProposalAccepted + …
74//! //  ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
75//! //  the statements this proof consumes
76//! ```
77//!
78//! That shape buys five checks with no bespoke tooling: unique identifiers (Rust name
79//! resolution), existence of every cited statement and every cited Rust item
80//! (`rustdoc::broken_intra_doc_links`, denied in CI), acyclicity of the dependency graph
81//! (`rustc`, `E0391`), and a rendered, clickable dependency list on each statement's page. There
82//! is deliberately no numbering: names are stable under insertion, and a stale citation is a
83//! build failure rather than a silently wrong cross reference. The same goes for the sections
84//! above — they are ordered, not numbered, so adding or splitting one renames nothing and
85//! invalidates no reference.
86//!
87//! Each statement opens with one of six labels, all of which mean something on their own:
88//!
89//! | label | carries a proof | meaning |
90//! |---|---|---|
91//! | **Definition** | no | fixes a term, and pins it to the Rust it denotes |
92//! | **Assumption** | no | something the implementation does not establish and a deployment must supply |
93//! | **Invariant** | yes | holds in every reachable state, proved by induction over transitions |
94//! | **Lemma** | yes | a proved statement |
95//! | **Theorem** | yes | one of the results the specification exists to establish |
96//! | **Remark** / **Caveat** | no | an observation or a limitation, asserting nothing new |
97//!
98//! None of them is *relational*: nothing is labelled by what it follows from. These pages are
99//! reached from anywhere by link, so there is no preceding result for a label to refer back to —
100//! and the supertrait list already names what a statement follows from, exactly and checkably.
101//!
102//! # The safety/liveness split
103//!
104//! The two arguments are deliberately disjoint in their assumptions, and the module layout
105//! reflects that:
106//!
107//! ```text
108//!  MaxByzantineWeight, UnforgeableSignatures,           EventualSynchrony, ClockAccuracy,
109//!  DurablePersistence, SequentialChainState,            CorrectValidatorAvailability,
110//!  EpochAgreement, DeterministicExecution               ActiveCorrectDriver, LeaderFairness,
111//!         |                                             RoundTimeoutGrowth, FullReachability
112//!         v                                                     |
113//!   quorum properties                                           |
114//!         |                                                     |
115//!         v                                                     v
116//!   voting rules  -->  rounds  -->  locking  -->  commit  -->  progress
117//!                                       |                        |
118//!                                       v                        v
119//!                                    SAFETY                   LIVENESS
120//!                              CommitAgreement            UnboundedProgress
121//!                                       |
122//!                                       v
123//!                                 ACCOUNTABILITY
124//!                                AccountableSafety
125//! ```
126//!
127//! Everything in the right-hand column can fail — the network can partition forever, every client
128//! can go away, clocks can drift — without endangering [`CommitAgreement`]. Nothing in the
129//! left-hand column can fail without endangering it. Accountability hangs below safety rather
130//! than above it: it consumes *fewer* assumptions than [`CommitAgreement`] does, because its job
131//! is to hold in the one regime where safety does not.
132//!
133//! # Known gaps
134//!
135//! The specification records, rather than papers over, the places where the implementation does
136//! not quite discharge what a proof wants. In rough order of significance:
137//!
138//! * [`FullReachability`] — the lock-recovery step wants the proposer to reach *every* correct
139//!   validator, while `synchronize_chain_state` guarantees only a quorum plus a grace period.
140//!   Affects liveness only.
141//! * [`MissingDependenciesAreRecoverable`] — a block that consumes a message or reads an event
142//!   depends on data originating on a *third* chain. Blobs, ancestors and chain state are
143//!   self-suppliable, so a lagging validator is simply handed them; these two classes are not. If
144//!   the proposer does not follow the sending or publishing chain either, the validator waits on
145//!   its own catch-up of that chain, which no assumption bounds — so
146//!   [`ValidationQuorumForms`]'s `2Δ` step does not apply to such blocks. Affects liveness only.
147//! * [`AccountabilityScope`] — incorrect block execution is not attributable, and its effects are
148//!   not confined to one chain: a wrong `messages` or `events` field is consumed by other chains
149//!   whose resulting blocks are themselves properly certified. The protections are
150//!   [`CertifiedBlockWasExecuted`] and [`IncomingBundlesAreSelfDerived`], and unlike the
151//!   accountability results both need [`MaxByzantineWeight`]. Tracked in
152//!   [issue #6675](https://github.com/linera-io/linera-protocol/issues/6675).
153//! * [`FastRetryPreservesBlock`] — closing the gap between "same proposal" and "same block" for a
154//!   fast-round retry relies on [`DeterministicExecution`] rather than on a runtime check at the
155//!   retry. Affects safety, and is the one step that reaches outside consensus into execution.
156//! * [`ProposalGate`] — several safety-critical guards live at the call site in
157//!   `chain_worker::state` rather than inside the [`ChainManager`] methods they protect; in
158//!   particular [`create_final_vote`] would sign twice in a round if invoked directly.
159//! * [`SafetyStateRecovery`] — the correspondence between a [`ManagerSafetySnapshot`] and the
160//!   instance it is restored into rests on a height check at the single call site, not on
161//!   anything the type enforces.
162//! * [`VoteConstructionSites`] — an exhaustive-search argument over the five signing sites, which
163//!   a sixth would invalidate.
164//!
165//! # Coverage
166//!
167//! Established today: agreement on the *sequence of blocks* of a single microchain, and what a
168//! certified block guarantees to nodes that were absent when it was certified.
169//!
170//! Not yet covered, in the sense that no statement here constrains them. Where consensus does say
171//! something adjacent, it is named.
172//!
173//! * **State-transition correctness** — that executing the agreed blocks yields the right state.
174//!   [`DeterministicExecution`] is assumed rather than proved, and *termination* of execution is
175//!   not stated at all. What is guaranteed is that a certified block was executed by some correct
176//!   validator ([`CertifiedBlockWasExecuted`]), that a voter matched every consumed bundle against
177//!   its own inbox ([`IncomingBundlesAreSelfDerived`]), and that the inputs execution needs can be
178//!   supplied to a validator lacking them ([`MissingDependenciesAreRecoverable`]), and that its
179//!   outputs — published blobs, events and the certificate — reach storage before the block counts
180//!   as processed ([`BlockOutputsArePersisted`]).
181//! * **Cross-chain messaging** as a subsystem — delivery in particular: nothing states that an
182//!   outbox is ever drained, so no bundle is guaranteed to arrive. What *is* stated is that an
183//!   inbox holds only bundles its origin really sent ([`InboxHoldsOnlySentBundles`]), that no two
184//!   blocks consume the same bundle ([`BundleConsumedAtMostOnce`]), and that each chain's own
185//!   block sequence is unique ([`UniqueChain`]).
186//! * **Committee reconfiguration** — epoch changes are agreed *by* this protocol on the admin
187//!   chain; [`EpochAgreement`] records what is assumed about them.
188//! * **Chain ownership and lifecycle** — who may propose at a height, and how that changes;
189//!   [`ConsensusInstance`] records what is assumed about it.
190//! * **Resource control and fees** — metering, declared block limits, and fee conservation.
191//! * **Event streams** as a subsystem — append-only-ness and the publisher-side guarantees behind
192//!   a cross-chain `OracleResponse::Event` read.
