4//! # The Linera protocol: correctness specification
5//!
6//! Linera is a multi-chain protocol: state is partitioned into *microchains*, each running its own
7//! consensus instance per block height. No chain can read another's state; they communicate by
8//! explicit message passing and through shared immutable stores — today, published blobs and events.
9//! This crate is the entry point to a correctness specification of that protocol — its system
10//! model, its assumptions, and proofs of the properties it is meant to have.
11//!
12//! The specification is written subsystem by subsystem; [Coverage](#coverage) says what is
13//! established today and what is not yet constrained by any statement here.
14//!
15//! **This crate contains no code.** The statements live next to the implementation they describe —
16//! today across [`linera_chain`] and [`linera_core`] — and this crate exists only so that a single
17//! index can link to all of them. Neither of those crates can do that on its own —
18//! `linera-core` depends on `linera-chain`, so the chain crate cannot name the progress and
19//! liveness results, and an index living there would have to describe half the specification in
20//! prose. Sitting above both, this crate can cite every statement by path, so every cross
21//! reference below is checked by the documentation build.
22//!
23//! ```bash
24//! cargo doc -p linera-spec --open
25//! ```
26//!
27//! # Headline results
28//!
29//! Three results anchor the consensus core, each concerning one microchain's sequence of blocks.
30//!
31//! * **Safety** — [`CommitAgreement`]: for any chain and height, all valid confirmed block
32//!   certificates certify the same block. No synchrony, availability or fairness assumption is
33//!   used; only [`MaxByzantineWeight`] and the cryptographic and persistence assumptions.
34//! * **Accountability** — [`AccountableSafety`]: if agreement *does* fail, the two conflicting
35//!   certificates alone convict validators of weight at least [`validity_threshold`] — more than
36//!   [`MaxByzantineWeight`] permits — and no correct validator is ever convictable. It assumes
37//!   strictly less than safety does, since it must hold precisely where safety does not.
38//! * **Liveness** — [`UnboundedProgress`]: with an active correct client and after GST, every
39//!   correct reachable validator's [`ChainTipState::next_block_height`] grows without bound.
40//!

65//! # How to read a statement
66//!
67//! Every claim is a public marker trait with no members, no implementors and no runtime
68//! footprint. Its **name is its identity**; its doc comment carries the statement and, unless it
69//! is a definition or an assumption, a proof. **Supertraits are proof dependencies**: a statement
70//! lists as supertraits exactly the earlier statements whose truth its proof consumes.
71//!
72//! ```text
73//! pub trait RoundProgress:
74//!     EventuallyCorrectLeader + LockRecovery + ProposalAccepted + …
75//! //  ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
76//! //  the statements this proof consumes
77//! ```
78//!
79//! That shape buys five checks with no bespoke tooling: unique identifiers (Rust name
80//! resolution), existence of every cited statement and every cited Rust item
81//! (`rustdoc::broken_intra_doc_links`, denied in CI), acyclicity of the dependency graph
82//! (`rustc`, `E0391`), and a rendered, clickable dependency list on each statement's page. There
83//! is deliberately no numbering: names are stable under insertion, and a stale citation is a
84//! build failure rather than a silently wrong cross reference. The same goes for the sections
85//! above — they are ordered, not numbered, so adding or splitting one renames nothing and
86//! invalidates no reference.
87//!
88//! Each statement opens with one of six labels, all of which mean something on their own:
89//!
90//! | label | carries a proof | meaning |
91//! |---|---|---|
92//! | **Definition** | no | fixes a term, and pins it to the Rust it denotes |
93//! | **Assumption** | no | something the implementation does not establish and a deployment must supply |
94//! | **Invariant** | yes | holds in every reachable state, proved by induction over transitions |
95//! | **Lemma** | yes | a proved statement |
96//! | **Theorem** | yes | one of the results the specification exists to establish |
97//! | **Remark** / **Caveat** | no | an observation or a limitation, asserting nothing new |
98//!
99//! None of them is *relational*: nothing is labelled by what it follows from. These pages are
100//! reached from anywhere by link, so there is no preceding result for a label to refer back to —
101//! and the supertrait list already names what a statement follows from, exactly and checkably.
102//!
103//! # The safety/liveness split
104//!
105//! Safety and liveness are deliberately disjoint in their assumptions, and the module layout
106//! reflects that. A third group of results sits below both and draws on each.
107//!
108//! ```text
109//!  MaxByzantineWeight, UnforgeableSignatures,           EventualSynchrony, ClockAccuracy,
110//!  DurablePersistence, SequentialChainState,            CorrectValidatorAvailability,
111//!  StorageAtomicity, EpochAgreement,                    ActiveCorrectDriver, LeaderFairness,
112//!  DeterministicExecution                               RoundTimeoutGrowth, FullReachability
113//!         |                                                     |
114//!         v                                                     |
115//!   quorum properties                                           |
116//!         |                                                     |
117//!         v                                                     v
118//!   voting rules  -->  rounds  -->  locking  -->  commit  -->  progress
119//!                                       |                        |
120//!                                       v                        v
121//!                                    SAFETY                   LIVENESS
122//!                              CommitAgreement            UnboundedProgress
123//!                                    |  |                        |
124//!                                    v  |                        |
125//!                              ACCOUNTABILITY                    |
126//!                             AccountableSafety                  |
127//!                                       |                        |
128//!                                       +-----------+------------+
129//!                                                   |
130//!                                                   v
131//!                                             AVAILABILITY
132//!                                 storage, checkpoints, messaging,
133//!                                    notifications, convergence
134//!                                  (+ BlobRetention, BoundedRecovery)
135//! ```
136//!
137//! **The two columns are independent.** Everything in the right-hand one can fail — the network can
138//! partition forever, every client can go away, clocks can drift — without endangering
139//! [`CommitAgreement`]. Nothing in the left-hand one can fail without endangering it. Accountability
140//! hangs below safety rather than above it: it consumes *fewer* assumptions than [`CommitAgreement`]
141//! does, because its job is to hold in the one regime where safety does not.
142//!
143//! **The third group is not a third column, because it spans both.** What a certified block
144//! guarantees to a node that was absent is a question about storage *and* about reachability:
145//! [`CertifiedBlockIsAvailable`] takes [`BlockOutputsArePersisted`] — hence [`StorageAtomicity`],
146//! from the left — together with [`CorrectValidatorAvailability`] and [`EventualSynchrony`] from the
147//! right. [`StorageConvergesAtEqualHeights`] is the sharpest case: it
148//! needs [`MaxByzantineWeight`] and [`StorageAtomicity`] to fix what two correct validators must
149//! agree on, and a *liveness*-shaped quiescence hypothesis that it explicitly cannot discharge.
150//!
151//! So a result in this group may be weakened by a failure in either column, and none of it protects
152//! [`CommitAgreement`] — which is the property the split exists to isolate.
153//!
154//! # Known gaps
155//!
156//! Two places where the implementation does not deliver what a proof wants. Each needs a change
157//! to the code, or a weaker result, to close; none is closed by rereading.
158//!
159//! * [`FullReachability`] — the lock-recovery step wants the proposer to reach *every* correct
160//!   validator, while `synchronize_chain_state` guarantees only a quorum plus a grace period.
161//!   Affects liveness only.
162//! * [`AccountabilityScope`] — incorrect block execution is not attributable, and its effects are
163//!   not confined to one chain: a wrong `messages` or `events` field is consumed by other chains
164//!   whose resulting blocks are themselves properly certified. The protections are
165//!   [`CertifiedBlockWasExecuted`] and [`IncomingBundlesMatchTheLocalInbox`], and unlike the
166//!   accountability results both need [`MaxByzantineWeight`]. Tracked in
167//!   [issue #6675](https://github.com/linera-io/linera-protocol/issues/6675).
168//!
169//! # Arguments that rest on the current shape of the code
170//!
171//! These are not gaps. Each is sound as it stands, but holds because of how the code is arranged
172//! today rather than by construction, so a change of the right kind invalidates the *argument*
173//! without any statement becoming visibly wrong. They are listed so that change can be recognized
174//! when it happens.
175//!
176//! | statement | what would invalidate it |
177//! |---|---|
178//! | [`VoteConstructionSites`] | a sixth signing site; the argument is an exhaustive search over the five that exist |
179//! | [`ProposalGate`] | a new caller of the [`ChainManager`] methods it protects — the guards live at the call site in `chain_worker::state`, so [`create_final_vote`] would sign twice in a round if invoked directly |
180//! | [`SafetyStateRecovery`] | a second restore site; the correspondence between a [`ManagerSafetySnapshot`] and the instance it is restored into rests on a height check at the one call site, not on anything the type enforces |
181//!
182//! The last two describe a public method whose precondition is met by convention rather than by
183//! the type. That is a latent hazard as well as a maintenance obligation — the same shape as
184//! [issue #6686](https://github.com/linera-io/linera-protocol/issues/6686), which tracks it for
185//! `apply_confirmed_block`.
186//!
187//! # Coverage
188//!
189//! Established today, in four areas:
190//!
191//! * **Agreement** on the *sequence of blocks* of a single microchain, with its accountability
192//!   converse and its progress counterpart — the three headline results above.
193//! * **Availability** — what a certified block guarantees to nodes that were absent when it was
194//!   certified, and what a crash costs.
195//! * **Conservation across a checkpoint** — that events, messages, blobs and execution state behave
196//!   as they would have without one ([`linera_chain::proof::checkpoints`]).
197//! * **The grounding of committee knowledge** — that no committee certifies its own introduction,
198//!   which is what makes induction on the epoch legitimate ([`CommitteeKnowledgeIsWellFounded`]).
199//! * **Storage validity** — what entitles a reader to act on stored bytes, by the kind of validity
200//!   proof each carries, and the one result about two validators rather than one: at equal heights
201//!   they agree on everything the protocol determines ([`StorageConvergesAtEqualHeights`]).
202//!
203//! Client notifications are specified too ([`linera_core::proof::notifications`]), but as a channel
204//! the model treats as lossy rather than as a guarantee anything rests on.
205//!
206//! Not yet covered, in the sense that no statement here constrains them. Where consensus does say
207//! something adjacent, it is named.
208//!
209//! * **State-transition correctness** — that executing the agreed blocks yields the right state.
210//!   [`DeterministicExecution`] is assumed rather than proved, and *termination* of execution is
211//!   not stated at all. What is guaranteed is that a certified block was executed by some correct
212//!   validator ([`CertifiedBlockWasExecuted`]), that a voter matched every consumed bundle against
213//!   its own inbox ([`IncomingBundlesMatchTheLocalInbox`]), and that the inputs execution needs can be
214//!   supplied to a validator lacking them ([`MissingDependenciesAreRecoverable`]), and that its
215//!   outputs — published blobs, events and the certificate — reach storage before the block counts
216//!   as processed ([`BlockOutputsArePersisted`]).
217//!
218//!   Safety reaches into execution in exactly one place: [`FastRetryPreservesBlock`] closes the
219//!   step from "same proposal" to "same block" with [`DeterministicExecution`] rather than a
220//!   runtime check at the retry. Everywhere else the two are kept apart.
221//! * **Cross-chain messaging** — largely covered, except for unconditional delivery. An inbox holds
222//!   only bundles its origin really sent ([`InboxHoldsOnlySentBundles`]); a voter matches every
223//!   consumed bundle against its own inbox ([`IncomingBundlesMatchTheLocalInbox`]); no two blocks
224//!   consume the same bundle ([`BundleConsumedAtMostOnce`]); delivery and consumption follow cursor
225//!   order, and only an entirely skippable bundle may be passed over
226//!   ([`DeliveryAndConsumptionAreOrdered`]); a sender may drop what its recipient has checkpointed
227//!   ([`AcknowledgedMessagesMayBeForgotten`]), though inbox entries themselves are never reclaimed
228//!   ([`InboxEntriesAreNeverReclaimed`]).
229//!
230//!   What is *not* guaranteed is that a bundle arrives at all. A sender's effort is bounded and a
231//!   recipient must ask for what it needs ([`DeliveryIsRepairedOnDemand`]); an idle sending chain
232//!   never retries a failed delivery, which is
233//!   [issue #6799](https://github.com/linera-io/linera-protocol/issues/6799).
234//! * **Committee reconfiguration** — partly covered now. [`CommitteeKnowledgeIsWellFounded`] fixes
235//!   where a node's knowledge of a committee comes from, and [`MaxByzantineWeight`] is assumed of
236//!   every epoch whose committee has not been revoked, so the fault bound accumulates as epochs are
237//!   created. What remains assumed is that the committee *for* an epoch is agreed, which
238//!   [`EpochAgreement`] records; and revocation is not usable in practice, so no committee is ever
239//!   retired and that accumulation never stops.
240//! * **Chain ownership and lifecycle** — who may propose at a height, and how that changes;
241//!   [`ConsensusInstance`] records what is assumed about it.
242//! * **Resource control and fees** — metering, declared block limits, and fee conservation.
243//! * **Event streams** — the reader side is covered, the publisher side is not. A voter resolves
244//!   every event a block reads against its own storage rather than trusting the proposer
245//!   ([`EventReadsResolveLocally`]); an event is written only under the publishing block's
246//!   certification, so it inherits that proof rather than carrying one
247//!   ([`AdmissionChecksTheValidityProof`]); and a stream's readable indices across a checkpoint are
248//!   fixed by [`EventFloorTracksCheckpoints`], with summarization by
249//!   [`CheckpointSummarizesUserStreams`].
250//!
251//!   Unstated: that a stream is append-only and gap-free in general — contiguity is claimed only
252//!   from a stream's floor upward — and what an application may assume about the *ordering* of
253//!   events it subscribes to across chains.
