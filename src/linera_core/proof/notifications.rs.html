4//! What a notification tells a client, and what it does not.
5//!
6//! A [`Notification`] is a hint that shortens the wait between a change and a client noticing it.
7//! From a correct validator it is sound — what it reports really happened — and from any validator
8//! it is best effort, since it may never arrive and carries nothing that could be checked if it
9//! did. The results here fix each half, and together they are the reason for the third: nothing in
10//! this specification may depend on a notification.
11//!
12//! [`Notification`]: crate::worker::Notification
13
14use linera_chain::manager::proof::model::{CorrectValidator, StorageAtomicity};
15
16use super::availability::{BlockOutputsArePersisted, InboxHoldsOnlySentBundles};

18/// **Lemma (A correct validator's notification reports a change that is already persisted).** If a
19/// client receives a [`Notification`] from a correct validator, the state change it names is in
20/// that validator's storage. Nothing is claimed about a faulty validator's notifications, which by
21/// [`CorrectValidator`] may report anything at all.
22///
23/// *Proof.* Notifications are not sent where they are built. Each is pushed onto the
24/// `notifications` field of a [`NetworkActions`] value which the handler *returns*, and every
25/// handler that changes state calls `save()` before returning it — a failed save propagates with
26/// `?`, so the actions are dropped and nothing is dispatched. Site by site:
27///
28/// * `Reason::NewBlock` and `Reason::NewEvents` are pushed in
29///   `ChainWorkerState::execute_contiguous_block` *before* its `self.save()?`, so a save that
30///   fails discards them along with the actions that carry them.
31/// * `Reason::NewEvents` is pushed again in `preprocess_certified_block`, there *after* the save
32///   rather than before it, which reaches the same conclusion more directly.
33/// * `Reason::NewRound` is built inside `create_network_actions` from
34///   `ChainManager::current_round`, and travels out in the same returned value.
35/// * `Reason::NewIncomingBundle` is pushed in `WorkerState::handle_cross_chain_request` only once
36///   `process_cross_chain_update` has returned `CrossChainUpdateResult::Updated`, and that result
37///   crosses a `chain_write` batch, which takes the lock and saves once before replying.
38///
39/// The caller dispatches through `Notifier::notify_chain` on the returned value alone, so no path
40/// reaches a client without a completed save. By [`StorageAtomicity`] that save is all or nothing,
41/// so a client is never told about a half-written change. ∎
42///
43/// **A notification carries no evidence.** [`Notification`] is a `chain_id` and a [`Reason`]: no
44/// signature, no certificate, not even a field naming the sender. Validators are not asked to sign
45/// them.
46///
47/// Knowing *who* sent one is a deployment matter rather than a protocol one. Under
48/// `NetworkProtocol::Grpc(TlsConfig::Tls)` the transport authenticates which validator the stream
49/// came from, so a third party cannot inject notifications into it; `TlsConfig::ClearText` is an
50/// equally valid configuration, so the specification cannot assume even that. And where it does
51/// hold it authenticates the *origin*, never the content, and only to the client holding the
52/// connection — a client cannot show anyone else what a validator told it. A notification is
53/// therefore never evidence in the sense the [accountability results] use: none is convictable, and
54/// none can be forwarded as proof of anything.
55///
56/// So the qualifier above is not a technicality a client can discharge by inspection. A fabricated
57/// notification is indistinguishable *by content* from a sound one, and the only way to learn
58/// whether the change happened is to ask. What a client may take from one is that it is worth
59/// querying now, and never what the answer will be. That the named block is *the* block at that
60/// height, rather than one validator's idea of it, is [`CommitAgreement`]'s and not this lemma's.
61///
62/// [accountability results]: linera_chain::justification::proof
63///
64/// [`Notification`]: crate::worker::Notification
65/// [`NetworkActions`]: crate::worker::NetworkActions
66/// [`Reason`]: crate::worker::Reason
67/// [`CorrectValidator`]: linera_chain::manager::proof::model::CorrectValidator
68/// [`CommitAgreement`]: linera_chain::manager::proof::safety::CommitAgreement
69pub trait NotificationImpliesPersistedChange: CorrectValidator + StorageAtomicity {}

71/// **Caveat (Notifications are best effort).** A change can be persisted and its notification
72/// never reach any client. Three ways, none of them an error:
73///
74/// * *Nobody is listening.* `Notifier::notify_chain` looks the chain up in its sender map and
75///   returns immediately when there is no entry.
76/// * *The receiver went away.* A failed `sender.send` is ignored and the dead sender reaped; for
77///   the delivery notifiers, `notifier.send(())` failing is logged at debug and dropped.
78/// * *The process crashed in between.* [`NotificationImpliesPersistedChange`] orders the save
79///   before the dispatch, which leaves exactly this window: saved, not yet notified, gone.
80///
81/// Delivery is therefore neither retried nor acknowledged, and no notification is durable.
82pub trait NotificationsAreBestEffort {}
83
84/// **Invariant (No proof depends on a notification arriving).** No statement in this specification
85/// has the delivery of a [`Notification`] among its premises.
86///
87/// *Proof.* By inspection of the whole specification: no statement names a notification in its
88/// dependencies, and the progress argument reaches its conclusions without one. What drives a
89/// chain forward is [`ActiveCorrectDriver`] — a client that polls and retries — together with the
90/// `linera_core::updater` loops, which learn what a validator is missing from the *error it
91/// returns*, never from a notification. [`MissingDependenciesAreRecoverable`] is that argument, and
92/// nothing in it consults a notification. ∎
93///
94/// This is what makes both ways a notification can fail harmless. A lost one costs latency, never
95/// correctness and never progress. A *fabricated* one — which a faulty validator can send freely,
96/// notifications being unauthenticated — costs at most a wasted query, since the client acts on
97/// what it then reads and not on what it was told. It also fixes their role: notifications are an
98/// optimization over polling, and a client that ignores them entirely is slower but no less
99/// correct.
100///
101/// **Re-established, not preserved.** Like any exhaustive-search argument this one holds only for
102/// the statements that exist. A future statement that assumed a client learns of a change promptly
103/// would break it, and would have to introduce a delivery assumption to say so.
104///
105/// [`Notification`]: crate::worker::Notification
106/// [`ActiveCorrectDriver`]: super::assumptions::ActiveCorrectDriver
107/// [`MissingDependenciesAreRecoverable`]: super::availability::MissingDependenciesAreRecoverable
108pub trait NoProofDependsOnNotifications: NotificationsAreBestEffort {}

110/// **Lemma (A notification is backed by a certificate the validator can serve — except for a new
111/// round).** For every notification a correct validator emits other than `Reason::NewRound`, that
112/// validator holds a quorum-signed certificate establishing what the notification reports, and
113/// will hand it to anyone who asks. A recipient can therefore not merely learn that something
114/// happened but verify it, and carry the evidence to other validators.
115///
116/// *Proof.* Site by site, for the reasons a validator emits.
117///
118/// * `Reason::NewBlock` and `Reason::NewEvents` are reachable only through
119///   `ChainWorkerState::process_confirmed_block`, which verifies the [`ConfirmedBlockCertificate`]
120///   with `certificate.check` and writes it with `write_blobs_and_certificate` *before* dispatching
121///   to the path that emits them ([`BlockOutputsArePersisted`]). The certificate is in storage
122///   before the notification exists.
123/// * `Reason::NewIncomingBundle` is emitted once `process_cross_chain_update` reports
124///   `CrossChainUpdateResult::Updated`. By [`InboxHoldsOnlySentBundles`] that bundle reached the
125///   inbox from another worker of the *same validator*, built from its persisted outbox for a block
126///   that validator had processed — so the sending block's certificate is in this validator's
127///   storage too. Note it certifies a block of the *sending* chain, not of the chain the
128///   notification names.
129///
130/// Serving them is the ordinary node surface: `download_certificate`, `download_certificates` and
131/// `download_certificates_by_heights`. A node that receives one of these notifications can fetch
132/// the certificate, verify it against the committee for its epoch, and push it onward —
133/// `send_confirmed_certificate`
134/// is exactly that path. ∎
135///
136/// This is the precise sense in which a notification is worth acting on despite carrying no
137/// evidence itself. The message is unsigned and, at best, authenticated only to its recipient by
138/// the transport; what it points at is quorum-signed and transferable to anyone. The hint is
139/// non-transferable, the thing it hints at is not.
140///
141/// **`Reason::NewRound` has no such backing, and cannot.** `ChainManager::update_current_round`
142/// takes a maximum over four inputs, and only two are certificates: a [`TimeoutCertificate`], or a
143/// locking block, which is a [`ValidatedBlockCertificate`]. The other two are `proposed` and
144/// `signed_proposal` — one owner's signature, not a quorum's. A round raised by a proposal in a
145/// higher multi-leader round therefore has nothing portable behind it, which is
146/// [`MultiLeaderRoundsAreLocal`] seen from the notification side: a recipient that wants to reach
147/// that round must be sent the proposal itself, because no compact proof of it exists to send.
148///
149/// **`Reason::BlockExecuted` is out of scope here.** It is emitted by `linera_core::client`, not by
150/// a validator, so it is a client telling itself something rather than a claim one node makes to
151/// another.
152///
153/// [`ConfirmedBlockCertificate`]: linera_chain::types::ConfirmedBlockCertificate
154/// [`ValidatedBlockCertificate`]: linera_chain::types::ValidatedBlockCertificate
155/// [`TimeoutCertificate`]: linera_chain::types::TimeoutCertificate
156/// [`MultiLeaderRoundsAreLocal`]: linera_chain::manager::proof::timeouts::MultiLeaderRoundsAreLocal
157pub trait NotificationIsCertificateBacked:
158    NotificationImpliesPersistedChange + BlockOutputsArePersisted + InboxHoldsOnlySentBundles
